# Editorial content rules

The visual system can be expressive. The copy stays clean.

## Core rule

Write like the client speaks when they know exactly what they mean. Precision over poetry. Concrete over promotional. Beauty should come from the idea, imagery, typography and composition — not from copy trying to sound beautiful.

## Default voice

- Clear, specific, restrained and human.
- Short when a short sentence is enough.
- Slightly editorial, never advertising-heavy.
- Confident without sounding self-important.
- Warm only when the business genuinely calls for warmth.
- Use the client's vocabulary; do not impose generic luxury, wellness, creative-agency or startup language.

## Avoid

- Empty promises: “elevate”, “transform”, “unforgettable journey”, “where passion meets purpose”.
- Wellness clichés, fake poetry and vague emotional claims.
- Generic luxury language such as “exceptional”, “timeless”, “curated” when it says nothing concrete.
- Headlines written only to sound clever.
- Long introductions before useful information.
- Repeating the same idea in heading, paragraph and CTA.
- Making every section sound important.

## Editing test

For every sentence ask:

1. Does it say something concrete?
2. Would this client actually say it?
3. Does it answer the visitor's current question?
4. Does it fit the visual tone of this project?
5. Can 20–30% be removed without losing meaning?
6. Is it trying harder to sound good than to be useful?

If the last answer is yes, rewrite it.

## Story before copy

Content follows the decision journey: audience → desire → friction → promise → proof → next action. Every section must answer a question the visitor needs answered at that point. If a section cannot justify its place in that sequence, remove or move it.

Conversion does not mean more CTAs. It means making the next sensible action obvious once the visitor has enough information and confidence.
